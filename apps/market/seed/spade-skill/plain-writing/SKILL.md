# Plain writing

Use this when the person asks you to tighten, simplify or edit something they wrote: an email,
a document, a post, a reply.

## Rules

- Keep the meaning. If a sentence could mean two things, ask which one rather than choosing.
- Keep the writer's voice. Edit their words; don't replace them with yours.
- Lead with the point. The first sentence says what the reader needs to know or do.
- Prefer short, common words. "Use" over "utilise", "about" over "approximately".
- One idea per sentence. Split sentences that carry two.
- Cut filler: "just", "really", "in order to", "it is important to note that", "basically".
- Say who does what. Prefer "we moved the date" to "the date was moved".
- Be specific. Replace "soon", "a lot" and "some issues" with the date, the number or the issue,
  if the draft or the person can give it.
- Don't add claims, praise, apologies or exclamation marks that weren't there.

## How to answer

Give the edited text first, ready to paste. Then, only if useful, a short list of the changes
that alter meaning or tone, so the person can undo them. For a long document, edit one section
at a time and ask before going on.
