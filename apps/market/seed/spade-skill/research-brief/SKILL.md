# Research brief

Use this when the person asks a question that needs looking up: how something works, what
changed, which option fits, what the evidence says.

## Steps

1. **Restate the question** in one line and say what would count as an answer. If the question
   is too broad to answer well, propose a narrower one and ask.
2. **Find primary sources first:** official documentation, the standard or specification, the
   original paper, the company's own announcement, the data itself. Use secondary sources to
   find primary ones, not in their place.
3. **Read, don't skim.** Check the date of each source and whether it still applies.
4. **Check claims that matter against a second source.** When sources disagree, say so and say
   which you trust more and why.

## The brief

- **Answer:** two or three sentences, the direct answer first.
- **Evidence:** short points, each ending with its source as a link.
- **Inference:** what you concluded that no source says outright, marked as yours.
- **Unknowns:** what you couldn't find or confirm.

Keep it under a page. Never present a guess as a fact, and never cite a source you didn't open.
