# Meeting minutes

Use this when the person hands you a transcript, a recording's text or their own notes from a
meeting and wants something they can send or file.

## What to produce

Write these sections, in this order, and leave out any that would be empty:

1. **Decisions.** One line each, stated as settled ("We ship the beta on 3 March"). Only what
   the meeting actually agreed; a suggestion nobody took up isn't a decision.
2. **Actions.** One line each: who, what, by when. If the notes don't say who or when, write
   "owner?" or "date?" rather than guessing.
3. **Open questions.** What was raised and left unanswered, with who is expected to answer if
   that was said.
4. **Notes.** Only context someone absent would need to understand the decisions. Keep it
   short.

## How to work

- Read the whole input before writing. Later parts often change what was decided earlier.
- Use the names people used. Don't invent titles or teams.
- Quote numbers, dates and figures exactly as said. If two disagree, list both and say so.
- Keep the minutes under a page unless the person asks for more.
- End with the meeting's date and who attended, if the input says.

## Before sending

Ask the person to check the actions before you send minutes to anyone else. Never send them
without the person's word.
